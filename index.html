Where the world’s finest beans grow

Coffee only thrives in the “bean belt” — the tropical band between 23.4°N and 23.4°S, where altitude, rainfall and volcanic soil shape every cup. Scroll to travel west to east.

    Hawaiʻi, USA

    Kona District · Hawaiʻi Island

    One of the rare coffees grown in the United States, on the volcanic slopes of Mauna Loa and Hualālai.

    Altitude150–900 m

    • Brown sugar
    • Milk chocolate

    Blue Mountains

    • Floral

    Colombia

    Huila · Southern Andes

    Andean valleys and two harvests a year make Huila the heart of Colombian specialty coffee.

    Altitude1,500–2,000 m
    • Red apple
    • Caramel
    • Citrus
  4. 0421.5°S · 45.5°W

    Brazil

    Sul de Minas · Minas Gerais

    The world’s largest producer. Natural-processed lots bring body and sweetness to espresso blends.

    Altitude800–1,300 m
    • Milk chocolate
    • Hazelnut
    • Low acidity
  5. 056.2°N · 38.2°E

    Ethiopia

    Yirgacheffe · Gedeo Zone

    The birthplace of coffee. Heirloom varieties deliver tea-like, intensely floral cups.

    Altitude1,750–2,200 m
    • Jasmine
    • Bergamot
    • Lemon
  6. 060.4°S · 37.0°E

    Kenya

    Nyeri · Central Highlands

    Volcanic soils on the slopes of Mount Kenya give SL28 and SL34 coffees a bright, juicy acidity.

    Altitude1,500–1,900 m
    • Blackcurrant
    • Grapefruit
    • Brown sugar
  7. 074.6°N · 96.8°E

    Indonesia

    Gayo Highlands · Aceh, Sumatra

    Wet-hulled processing (giling basah) creates the heavy body and earthy depth Sumatra is known for.

    Altitude1,200–1,600 m
    • Dark chocolate
    • Cedar
    • Earthy spice
